# Growing your career (day 60 to 90 and beyond)

> Synthetic document for the LaunchPad prototype. Not real policy.

## Your 6-month probation review
Probation lasts **6 months**, with check-ins at **1, 3 and 6 months**. At the **6-month review** your manager:
- Looks back at your objectives, your development plan and how you've lived our values.
- Confirms that you've **passed probation**, and you'll get written confirmation in the HR portal.
- In rare cases, extends probation (usually by up to 3 months) with a clear plan and support. This should never be a surprise: concerns are raised at earlier check-ins.
After probation, you move onto the normal yearly cycle of objectives, mid-year and year-end reviews.

## Career conversations
- A career conversation is separate from your objectives. It's about **where you want to go**, not just what you're delivering now.
- Ask your manager for one at any time. Many people have their first one around **Day 90**.
- Useful questions: What do I enjoy most? What skills do I want to build? What roles interest me in 2 to 3 years?
- You own your career. Your manager's job is to support it.

## Moving roles internally
- Internal vacancies are advertised on the intranet jobs page.
- You can usually apply for internal roles **once you've passed probation** and been in your role for about **12 months**. Talk to your manager before you apply.
- Secondments and short-term projects are another way to try something new without changing job permanently.
- Your manager won't be told you've applied until you're shortlisted, but it's better to have the conversation with them first.

## Learning paths
- The learning portal has **learning paths**: curated sets of courses for skills such as data, cloud, leadership, coaching, customer experience and risk.
- Some paths lead to professional qualifications. Ask your manager about support for exam fees and study time.
- Add the paths you choose to your development plan (see *Setting goals and your development plan (days 30 to 60)*).

## Mentoring
- A mentor is an experienced colleague outside your reporting line who helps you think about your growth.
- Find a mentor through the **mentoring scheme on the intranet**, or ask your manager or buddy for an introduction.
- Most mentoring relationships meet for **about an hour a month** for 6 to 12 months.
- You can also become a reverse mentor: sharing your perspective with a senior leader.

## Colleague networks
Networks for gender, ethnicity, LGBTQ+, disability, carers, faith, armed forces and early careers are **open to everyone**, from your first day. Join through the intranet. Networks run events, speaker sessions and development opportunities, and they're a great way to meet people outside your team (see *Wellbeing, adjustments and support*).

## Volunteering
- Every colleague gets **2 paid volunteering days a year**, which you can use from your first year once your manager has agreed the dates.
- Use them for team volunteering, a local charity, or skills-based volunteering such as money confidence sessions in schools and community groups.
- Find opportunities and record your days on the intranet.
- Volunteering connects to our purpose of helping people and communities build a secure future (see *Our purpose*).

## Keeping momentum
- Keep regular 1:1s and keep your development plan up to date.
- Revisit your goals at mid-year, not just at year end.
- Share what you learn: the value **grow together** means helping others as well as yourself.
