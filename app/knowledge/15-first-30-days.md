# Your first 30 days, week by week

> Synthetic document for the LaunchPad prototype. Not real policy.

Your first month has a simple shape: get set up, get to know people, finish your mandatory training and agree what you'll focus on next. This guide shows what usually happens each week. Your manager may adjust it for your role.

## Week 1: setup and meeting your team
- **Day 1:** arrive at **9:30am**, collect your building pass and laptop, set up multi-factor authentication, have lunch with your team and join the "Welcome to the Bank" induction (see *Your first day and first week*).
- **Meet your team.** Your manager introduces you to the team in Week 1. Your teammates are your go-to for everyday questions like where things are, which channels to join and how meetings really work, so ask them freely.
- **Start your mandatory training** in the learning portal. You have **30 days** to finish all 7 modules, about **6 hours** in total, in work time (see *Mandatory training: what, when and how*).
- **1:1 with your manager** to agree your 30-day plan and any role training.
- **Check your desk and days.** Expect to be in the office **3 to 4 days a week** in your first 4 weeks. Book desks in the desk booking app (see *Hybrid working, locations and travel*).

## Week 2: finding your feet
- Keep going with mandatory training. A good target is to have **about half the modules done** by the end of Week 2.
- Shadow colleagues: sit in on team ceremonies, customer calls or code reviews, depending on your role.
- Set up a few short introductions with people your team works with most. Your manager or your team can suggest who.
- Check your benefits choices. Your **benefits window closes 30 days after your start date** (see *Pay and benefits*).
- Customer-facing colleagues continue their structured training programme, which runs for the first 4 to 6 weeks.

## Week 3: doing real work
- Pick up a first small piece of work with support: a small change, a few supervised calls, or a short piece of analysis.
- Ask for feedback on it. "What's one thing I could do differently next time?" is a good question to ask.
- Keep a short note of what you've learned, what's gone well and what's been hard. It makes your 30-day check-in much easier.
- If you're struggling to fit in training, tell your manager now rather than in Week 4.

## Week 4: finish training and prepare for your 30-day check-in
- **Finish all mandatory training** before Day 30. Your manager can see your progress in the learning portal.
- **Prepare for your 30-day check-in.** Think about:
  - How your first month has gone, and what would have helped.
  - Where you've seen our values in action (see *Our values*).
  - What you'd like to focus on and learn next.
- At the check-in you'll confirm your training is complete and agree your **first objectives** (see *Setting goals and your development plan (days 30 to 60)*).
- Your 30-day check-in is also the first of your probation check-ins. Probation lasts **6 months**, with check-ins at **1, 3 and 6 months**.

## If things don't go to plan
Leave, illness or a delayed laptop can push things back. That's fine. Tell your manager early and agree new dates together (see *When plans change*).
