# Mandatory training: what, when and how

> Synthetic document for the LaunchPad prototype. Not real policy.

## The basics
- Everyone completes **7 mandatory modules** in the learning portal **within 30 days of starting**.
- Together they take **about 6 hours**, and you do them **in work time**.
- Your manager can see your progress. Your 30-day check-in confirms it's all done (see *Learning and mandatory training*).
- The optional **pre-start onboarding training** (about 40 minutes) is separate and doesn't count towards these modules.

## Code of conduct (45 minutes)
Covers how we're expected to behave: conflicts of interest, gifts and hospitality, personal trading, social media and handling data. **Why it matters:** it protects customers, colleagues and you. See *Code of conduct essentials* for a summary.

## Data protection and information security (1 hour)
Covers how to handle customer and colleague data, spotting phishing, password and MFA habits, and what to do if data goes to the wrong place. **Why it matters:** customers trust us with their information, and one mistake can cause real harm.

## Financial crime: anti-money laundering, sanctions and fraud (1.5 hours)
The longest module. Covers how criminals try to use banks, warning signs of money laundering and fraud, sanctions rules, and how to report a suspicion. **Why it matters:** the law requires every colleague to know how to spot and report financial crime, whatever their role.

## Treating customers fairly and Consumer Duty (1 hour)
Covers delivering good outcomes for customers, fair value, clear communication and supporting customers in vulnerable circumstances. **Why it matters:** it's at the heart of our purpose (see *Our purpose*).

## Health, safety and wellbeing (30 minutes)
Covers fire safety, first aid, reporting accidents, setting up your workstation safely at home and in the office, and where to get support. **Why it matters:** so you stay safe and well wherever you work.

## Inclusion and respect at work (45 minutes)
Covers treating people with respect, recognising bias, and what to do about bullying or harassment. **Why it matters:** everyone should be able to be themselves at work.

## Speaking up (whistleblowing) (30 minutes)
Covers how to raise a concern with your manager or the confidential Speak Up line, and the protection you get for raising a genuine concern. **Why it matters:** problems can only be fixed if people feel safe to raise them.

## A suggested schedule
You can do the modules in any order, but this spreads them across your first 4 weeks:
- **Week 1:** Code of conduct, and Data protection and information security (1 hour 45 minutes).
- **Week 2:** Financial crime (1.5 hours).
- **Week 3:** Treating customers fairly and Consumer Duty, and Health, safety and wellbeing (1.5 hours).
- **Week 4:** Inclusion and respect at work, and Speaking up (1 hour 15 minutes).
Tip: block time in your Outlook calendar, about 30 to 60 minutes at a time. Modules save your progress, so you can stop and come back.

## If you fall behind or go overdue
- If you can't finish in 30 days, for example because of leave or illness, **talk to your manager** as early as you can. They can agree a new date.
- Overdue modules show as overdue to you and your manager in the learning portal, and you'll get reminder emails.
- Finishing your mandatory training is part of your **probation**, so don't leave it to the last week.

## Accessibility and adjustments
- Modules work with screen readers and keyboard navigation, and videos have captions and transcripts.
- If you need more time, a different format or other support, tell your manager. You don't have to share a diagnosis (see *Wellbeing, adjustments and support*).

## Refreshers every year
Mandatory training isn't a one-off. You'll complete **shorter refresher modules every year**, and the learning portal tells you when they're due. Some roles also have extra role-specific modules, which your manager will tell you about.
