# LBG systems and ways of working

> Synthetic document for the LaunchPad prototype. Not real policy. System names here are generic.

## Email, chat and calendar
- **Outlook** is for email and your calendar. **Microsoft Teams** is for chat, calls, meetings and team channels.
- Most day-to-day conversation happens in Teams. Use email for anything formal, external, or that needs a record.
- Your manager will add you to your team's channels in Week 1. Ask them which ones matter most.
- You can use the Teams and Outlook apps on your personal phone once you've set up MFA (see *Your laptop, accounts and IT*).

## The intranet
The intranet is the Bank's internal website. Use it for news, policies, team pages, the colleague directory and joining colleague networks. If you're not sure where something lives, search the intranet first.

## The HR portal
The HR portal is where you manage your working life:
- Payslips, pension and benefits (see *Pay and benefits*).
- Booking holiday and recording absence.
- **Claiming expenses**, within **60 days** and with receipts. They're paid with your next salary.
- Flexible working requests and updating your personal details.
For help, raise a request with **HR Service** through the portal.

## The learning portal
All mandatory training, role training and learning paths live in the learning portal. Your manager can see your progress (see *Mandatory training: what, when and how*).

## IT self-service
- Press the **IT Help** button on your laptop to reach the IT Service Desk, 24/7. You can reset your password, request software and report problems there.
- Report a lost or stolen laptop or phone **straight away**, at any time.

## Booking a desk
Offices use hot desking. Book in the **desk booking app**, up to **2 weeks ahead**. Teams sit together in "neighbourhoods" on their office days (see *Hybrid working, locations and travel*).

## How teams work
- Many teams, especially in technology and change, work in **agile** teams: small, cross-functional groups that plan work in short cycles (often 2 weeks) and review it together.
- Hybrid working is the norm: most office-based colleagues are in **at least 2 days a week**, averaged over a month, on days their team agrees.
- Contact centre and branch colleagues work set shifts from a fixed location.

## Meeting habits
- Every meeting should have a clear purpose and, ideally, an agenda in the invite.
- Default to **25 or 50 minutes** rather than 30 or 60, so people have a break between calls.
- Turn your camera on if you can, especially when you're new and people are learning who you are. It's fine not to.
- If a meeting has no purpose for you, it's OK to politely decline.
- Check people's working hours before booking: not everyone works 9 to 5.

## Common terms and acronyms
- **BAU:** business as usual, meaning normal day-to-day work rather than a project.
- **Stand-up:** a short daily team meeting to share progress and blockers.
- **Sprint:** a short, fixed period of work in an agile team, often 2 weeks.
- **Retro (retrospective):** a meeting at the end of a sprint to talk about what went well and what to change.
- **Backlog:** the list of work a team hasn't started yet.
- **Squad:** another name for a small agile team.
- **Stakeholder:** anyone affected by or interested in a piece of work.
- **1:1:** a regular private meeting with your manager.
- **OOO:** out of office.
- **EOD:** end of day.
- **KYC:** know your customer, the checks we do to confirm who a customer is.
- **AML:** anti-money laundering.
- **Consumer Duty:** the regulator's rules on delivering good outcomes for customers.
- **Vulnerable customer:** a customer whose circumstances make them more likely to come to harm, for example because of illness or money worries.
If you hear an acronym you don't know, ask. Someone else in the room is probably wondering too.

## Security habits
- **Lock your screen** whenever you leave your desk (Windows key + L).
- Wear your building pass in the office, and don't let people follow you through security doors.
- **Report suspicious emails** using the report phishing button in Outlook. Don't click links or open attachments you weren't expecting.
- **Nobody from the Bank will ever ask for your password.**
- Don't forward work email to personal accounts or store work files on personal devices.
- When working from home, make sure customer information can't be seen or overheard.
