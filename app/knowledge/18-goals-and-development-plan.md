# Setting goals and your development plan (days 30 to 60)

> Synthetic document for the LaunchPad prototype. Not real policy.

## Agreeing your first objectives
At your **30-day check-in**, you and your manager agree your first objectives for the year (see *Learning and mandatory training*).
- Aim for **three clear objectives**. Fewer, clearer goals are better than a long list.
- Your manager will explain how your objectives connect to the team's priorities and to our purpose.
- Performance is discussed on **what** you deliver and **how** you do it, in line with our values (see *Our values*).
- Objectives are recorded in the HR portal, so you can both see them and update them.

## Writing good goals
A good goal is **specific, measurable and has a date**. Ask yourself:
- What exactly will be different?
- How will we both know it's done?
- By when?
- Is it realistic for someone still learning the role?
"Learn the systems" is vague. "Complete the payments onboarding course and deliver one small change to production by Day 90" is clear.

## Example goals: software engineer
- Deliver **two small changes** to production, with code review, by Day 90.
- Complete the team's onboarding course and set up a local development environment by Day 45.
- Take part in every sprint ceremony and **lead one retro** by Day 90.
- Pair with a senior engineer at least once a week for your first 3 months.

## Example goals: contact centre colleague
- Complete the structured training programme and pass the assessment before taking calls on your own.
- Reach the team's quality score target on monitored calls by the end of Month 3.
- Use the vulnerable customer guidance on every relevant call, checked in monthly call reviews.
- Learn to handle **two new call types** with support by Day 90.

## Your development plan
Alongside your objectives, agree a simple development plan with your manager. It covers:
- **Skills to build** for your current role.
- **How you'll learn them:** courses in the learning portal, shadowing, pairing or stretch tasks.
- **Support you need**, for example time, a mentor or access to a system.
Keep it short: one page is plenty. A rough guide is 70% learning on the job, 20% from other people and 10% from courses.

## Your 3-month probation check-in
Probation lasts **6 months**, with check-ins at **1, 3 and 6 months**. At the **3-month check-in** your manager will:
- Review progress against your objectives and development plan.
- Talk about how you're living the values.
- Agree what to focus on before your 6-month review.
If anything is off track, it should never be a surprise. Your manager will raise concerns early and agree how they'll support you (see *Growing your career (day 60 to 90 and beyond)*).

## Feedback habits
- **Keep regular 1:1s** with your manager, usually every week or two. Bring your own agenda.
- **Ask for feedback little and often.** "What's one thing I did well, and one thing I could do better?" works after any piece of work.
- **Give feedback too.** Be specific and kind, and focus on the work, not the person.
- **Keep a short log** of wins, feedback and lessons. It makes check-ins quicker and fairer.
- Saying thank you counts. Recognising a colleague's help is part of growing together.
